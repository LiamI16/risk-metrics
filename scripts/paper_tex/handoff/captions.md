# Suggested captions

Numbers are computed from the same draw the figures plot. Macros used below (`\DCPA`,
`\TCPA`, `\VO`, `\prel`, `\vrel`) are the paper's own.

## Fig. equivalence

> Theorem 1 as a set identity in own-velocity space, for an encounter with
> $\norm{\prel} = 60$~m and $R = 20$~m. (a) $\DCPA \le R$ is invariant under
> $\vrel \mapsto -\vrel$ and is therefore a bowtie: the collision cone together with its
> mirror lobe. (b) $\TCPA > 0$ is a half-plane through the apex. (c) Their intersection is
> the cone, drawn here against the independent tangent construction. The two marked
> velocities are a time-reversed pair with identical $\DCPA$ and opposite-sign $\TCPA$:
> $\DCPA$ alone cannot separate them. Marks are 8{,}000 candidate own velocities drawn
> uniformly over the plotted range, shaded darker with larger magnitude. Used as a test of
> $\VO$ membership, $\DCPA \le R$ alone is wrong on 957 of them and $\TCPA > 0$ alone on
> 3{,}028; together they are wrong on none.

## Fig. stochastic (Gaussian)

> Two encounters under identical Gaussian uncertainty ($\sigma_p = 5$~m, $\sigma_v = 0.5$~m/s
> on the relative state), 5{,}000 samples each. Both are nominally inside the $\VO$, so the
> deterministic verdict is the same, yet the crossing collides with probability $0.93$ and
> the collinear overtake with probability $0.14$. $\TCPA$'s spread is what separates them:
> its interquartile range grows from 2~s to 102~s as the closing speed falls, while
> $\DCPA$ stays bounded by the current separation.

## Fig. stochastic (bounded)

> The same two encounters under bounded uncertainty of the same size: uniform ellipses whose
> hard edge is the $2\sigma$ contour of the Gaussian above (discs of radius 10~m and
> 1~m/s), so the two models carry the same covariance and differ only in the shape of the
> distribution. Collision probabilities are $0.93$ and $0.11$.

Note: with 5{,}000 samples, $P[\text{collision}]$ is good to about $\pm 0.01$, so it is
quoted to two decimals.
